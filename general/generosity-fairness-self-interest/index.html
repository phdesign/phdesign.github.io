Generosity, Fairness, and Self-Interest

posted on 20 October 2026 in general

This is just AI slop, but it’s great AI slop. When I asked this I was expecting some trite answer, but I was surprised by the depth of the answer and decided it’s worth sharing.

Is it better to be generous, to be fair, or to put yourself first?

The right choice depends on which action produces the greatest overall wellbeing and minimizes harm for everyone affected. None of these three approaches works as an absolute rule; each is best evaluated by its actual outcomes.

Generosity

Generosity delivers the highest net good when your resources, time, or energy can alleviate substantial suffering or significantly improve someone else’s life at a relatively low cost to yourself.

  • Where it wins: Donating excess income to high-impact charities or offering critical help to someone in crisis creates massive net positive value.
  • Where it fails: Constant self-sacrifice that leaves you exhausted, burnt out, or financially unstable reduces your long-term capacity to help anyone, creating a net negative outcome over time.

Fairness

Fairness serves as a vital baseline. Impartiality, justice, and consistent rules keep societies stable, build trust, and prevent exploitation, which maximizes collective stability and happiness.

  • Where it wins: Deciding how to distribute shared resources, setting public policy, or resolving disputes impartially ensures predictable, reliable outcomes for the group.
  • Where it fails: Rigid adherence to strict equality can yield poor results if it ignores specific needs. Equal distribution of resources to a wealthy person and a starving person produces far less total benefit than directing those resources where they relieve actual suffering.

Putting Yourself First

Prioritising yourself is essential, but primarily as a foundation that enables everything else. You cannot generate value for others if your own baseline needs are unmet.

  • Where it wins: Maintaining your physical health, mental well-being, and financial independence ensures you remain a net contributor to the world rather than someone who requires care from others.
  • Where it fails: Self-interest that crosses into pure selfishness, or comes at the direct expense of others’ well-being, decreases the net good in the world and causes avoidable harm.

The most effective approach is to view self-care as the foundation, fairness as the default rule for group harmony, and targeted generosity as the dynamic tool to maximize positive impact whenever the opportunity arises.
